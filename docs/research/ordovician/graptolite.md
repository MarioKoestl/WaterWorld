# Graptolite (drifting colony)

Sources: Digital Atlas of Ordovician Life (not verified here); general references on graptolite morphology.

## Form
- A colonial animal: a stipe (blade) with thecae (cups) along one or both sides
- Biserial form common in the Late Ordovician: teeth on both sides of the stipe
- Length: 2 to 10 cm (the whole colony)
- Stipe width: about 1.5 to 3 mm including teeth
- Stipe thickness: about 0.5 to 1 mm (organic periderm)
- Nema (thread): a thin filament at the proximal end, by which the colony hung
- Planktonic: drifted in the open water column

## Figures used in the mesh
- Modeled one unit long (from bottom of stipe to nema tip)
- Stipe: z=0 to z=0.82, as a slab with saw-tooth width profile
- Teeth: 8 to 10 per side, modeled as width variation of the slab
- Nema: z=0.82 to z=1.0, a thin tube
- Stipe base width: 1.5% of unit length, narrowing to tip
- Stipe half-thickness: 0.4% of unit length

## What stays an estimate
- Exact tooth shape and spacing (varies by genus)
- Stipe thickness (flattened in fossils)
- Nema length and thickness
- All colours (dark organic material assumed: brown to black periderm)
- Number of teeth per side
