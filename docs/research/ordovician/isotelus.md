# Isotelus (the trilobite Isotelus maximus), research notes

Written 2026-11-25, for the build after stage G of `ordovician-life.md`. Sources read today:

- Wikipedia, "Isotelus" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotelus), read 2026-11-25. Quotes below are word for word.
- Ohio Geological Survey, GeoFacts 6 (GF6_Shrake_2023.pdf), named by the brief for segments and eyes: the PDF was
  fetched but its text could not be extracted here. Everything it was to give comes from Wikipedia instead, or is
  marked estimate.
- Digital Atlas of Ordovician Life: the Isotelus pages tried returned 404. Not used.
- Sizes and the card: `era-ordovician.md` (Wikimedia Commons "up to 15 inches for complete Ohio specimens",
  Cincinnati Museum Center 70 cm relative). The card stays as it is.

## What the source prints

- Thorax: "The thorax is composed of eight segments of equal size", "smooth dorsally, though marked with small
  pits and occasionally shallow bumps". "Each of the 8 thorax segments would possess a single leg pair."
- Cephalon: "large, rounded, and mostly smooth", "with large short eyes". Glabella "was sloping and poorly
  defined". The axial furrow is "shallow and smooth".
- Pygidium: "expansive and mostly smooth, subtriangular in outline". "The axial and pleural lobes of the pygidium
  are poorly defined, with a shallow furrow dividing the border of the pygidium from the central portion."
- Genal spines: "The pair of thin genal spines at the corners of the cephalon are a diagnostic trait among
  species", in some species "may reach as far back as the pygidium"; I. maximus has them in all adult specimens
  (article's species section). I. maximus: "semi-circular cephalon and pygidium".
- Life: "carnivorous and a burrower which lived in warm shallow seas"; it "often buried itself in sediment, with
  only its eyes and portions of the dorsal exoskeleton exposed". Hypostome "large and forked", "with 2 large
  posteriorly-facing prongs", perhaps used to pry worms out of the sediment. "A pair of anteriorly directed
  antennae would have been present"; an Ohio specimen shows "at least 26 pairs of identical legs".
- Sizes: "I. maximus reaching up to at least 53 centimetres"; the Huffman Dam specimen 37 cm long and 25.5 cm
  wide (so width about 0.69 of length); I. rex holotype 720 mm long and 70 mm high (so height about 0.10 of
  length; from the article's tail, read less exactly).

## Measured on which figure, and what stays an estimate

- Width 0.69 of length: from the Huffman Dam figures above. Height 0.10 of length: from I. rex, carried over to
  I. maximus as an estimate.
- Head : thorax : tail = 35 : 30 : 35 of the length: estimate, from "semi-circular cephalon and pygidium" of
  about the same size with eight short segments between them. No source prints the ratio.
- Eyes: "large short eyes" (the brief's table says small; the source wins for the footprint, the "short" keeps
  them low). Holochroal: "small contiguous lenses set on a curving visual surface" (a review of trilobite work,
  title and author not verified, https://core.ac.uk/download/429714842.pdf); in the relative Asaphus that
  surface is a piece of an almost perfect spheroid (Clarkson, "The eyes of Asaphus raniceps Dalman (Trilobita)",
  abstract). Built after qa/ordovician.md I4 as a low tower out of each cheek: footprint a bean of 0.06 by 0.03,
  0.023 high, the outer wall the dark visual band, the top a flat lid of the shell's skin. All measures are
  estimates: no source gives them for Isotelus.
- Genal spines: flat blades, the head's rim drawn out backward, reaching to the fourth thoracic segment (Mario,
  qa/ordovician.md I2, 2026-11-25): an estimate inside the printed range (corner of the head to as far as the
  pygidium; they never reach the tail).
- Legs: 14 of the "at least 26 pairs" are modelled, the last at z = -0.40 of the length so none ever steps out
  from under the tail (qa I1); under the flat shield the missing ones cannot be told apart from outside. Leg
  length, the border width, the depth of every furrow, everything about colour: estimates, no source.
- No gills are modelled (the source has only "possible filaments"); no rear feelers (known from Olenoides, not
  from Isotelus).

## The living model (quality-gate.md)

The horseshoe crab, for surface and motion: a smooth domed shield gliding low and ploughing through mud, a dull
broad sheen, quiet uneven tone. The shape comes from the fossil, not from the crab. Colour is free (no source):
a plain olive-brown family, low saturation, hollows darker, worn edges paler, each animal its own tone (L5, L7, L8).

## The middle forms (2 to 10 % of the body, recipe step 1)

1. The flattened border: a concave band round cephalon and pygidium with the shallow furrow inside it (sourced
   for the pygidium, carried round the head as estimate), and a rounded edge with a thickness.
2. The eight thoracic segments: each a tile of its own with a low rib, its front edge tucked under the one in
   front, the joints shaded steps, not drawn lines (L3).
3. The faint axis: a broad low arch the furrows of which are shallow and smooth, fading on head and tail.
4. The raised eye crescents: a lens under a bowed lid on each cheek, dark (L6).
5. The thin genal spines, swept back along the sides, round with a taper.
6. The forked hypostome under the head: a plate with two prongs facing back.
7. The antennae, forward and out from under the front rim.
8. The legs with their feet, and the gap of light between body and ground.
