## 1. Length

- E2023 (confirmed by web search, Wikipedia): typical adult 3.4 m (CMNH 5768), largest known about 4.1 m
  (CMNH 5936). Error margins allow up to 3.7 m typical and 4.5 m exceptional.
- E2023b: orbit-opercular length method, 3169 observations, 972 species. Scaling Ferrón's reconstruction by
  CMNH 5768 head length (61.3 cm) gives 3.77 m, supporting shorter lengths.
- F2017 (outdated, NOT used for length): 6.88 m (CMNH 5768) to 8.79 m (CMNH 5936). E2023b calls these
  overestimates based on unreliable jaw-perimeter scaling.
- Weight (Wikipedia citing E2023): 950–1200 kg for a typical 3.41 m adult, 1494–1764 kg for the 4.1 m largest.
  Compare: a 4.6 m great white weighs about 2000 kg.

## 2. Head

- E2024 (from brief): from the eye to the gill cover about 0.18 TL.
- E2023b: in most non-anguilliform fishes the head is 18–30% of TL. In other arthrodires with complete remains
  about 20% TL. The old Ferrón reconstruction would put the head at only 8% — called unrealistic.
- Skull of CMNH 5768: 61.3 cm long (W). Skulls of the species reach 60–70 cm.
- EA (from Wikipedia): gape angle of at least 65–70°. More cartilage in the skull than usually assumed. The cheek
  is loose and flexible.
- Snout: rounded and blunt, no pointed nose (E2024, from brief). Two pairs of nostrils.
- Mouth (E2024, from brief): more soft tissue around it than usually drawn. The bone blades show only in part.

## 3. Trunk and body shape

- E2024 (from brief): ventral shield of the armour 0.30–0.32 TL. The armour ends at about 0.5 TL (the pelvic
  fins sit at the rear end of the ventral shield, the vent at about 0.5 TL).
- E2024 (from brief): greatest depth at the trunk armour 0.25–0.28 TL, in the largest mount 0.28–0.30.
- E2024 (from brief, confirmed by web search): depth against width 1.21–1.29 in adults (an oval that is deeper
  than wide). Young ones are nearly round. Specimens CMNH 6194 and CMNH 7424 are nearly circular (ratio close
  to 1).
- E2024 (from brief): belly curved, not flat. The flat belly of old drawings is called a mistake.
- E2024 (from brief): length without tail against depth 2.66–3.18.
- E2023b: arthrodires generally had relatively deeper and wider bodies than sharks. Active swimmers have a
  thorax subcircular in cross-section.
- Body as a whole (E2024, from brief): like a tuna or a mackerel shark, but with a blunt head, a larger front,
  fins further forward and below. The front half is stiff: it swims with the part behind the pelvic fins only.
- Press coverage (Engelman in Sci.news): "A 3.3-m-long Dunkleosteus terrelli is essentially the same weight as
  a 4.6-m (15 foot) great white shark." ScienceDaily: "a shorter body and shape of the body armor also meant
  Dunkleosteus was likely much chunkier."

## 4. Spine

- E2024 (from brief): height of the spine in the body at 0.66–0.75 of the body's height: high.
- W: only 16 vertebrae known (CMNH 50322), highly fused, extending nearly to the end of the trunk armor. The
  page suggests thunniform-like force transmission to the tail.

## 5. Fins

- **Pectoral:** E2024 (from brief): base begins 0.131 TL behind the snout (range 0.112–0.146). About half lies
  under the head, below the gill chamber. Base 0.085–0.10 TL long. Shape: pointed, like that of a mackerel
  shark, broad at the base, front edge straight, aspect ratio about 1.3, standing out at nearly 90° from the
  body. Span given as about twice the width of the chest.
- **Pelvic:** E2024 (from brief): at the rear end of the ventral shield, small. Girdle 0.042–0.060 TL high
  (confirmed: web search gives 6.0% and 5.7% for two specimens).
- **Dorsal:** E2024 (from brief): one. Begins right behind the keel of the median dorsal plate, as far forward
  as the armour allows, behind the centre of mass. Triangular, small, lower than a shark's. Its height is not
  known.
- **Anal:** E2024 (from brief): drawn, but with nothing of it preserved. Very uncertain.
- **Ceratotrichia:** an exceptionally preserved specimen shows pectoral fin ceratotrichia (W citing Carr et al.
  2010), suggesting fin shape varied more than thought.

## 6. Tail

- F2017 (read in full): a caudal fin with a well-developed ventral lobe, narrow peduncle and wide span. All
  specimens show heterocercal angles of 22–30° and hypocercal angles of 44–47°. The largest specimen has the
  most developed ventral lobe and highest angles (30°/47°).
- E2024 (from brief): a crescent, close to symmetrical, with a large lower lobe. Its span at least the depth
  of the trunk. No fossil of it exists: "the most speculative aspect" of the reconstruction.
- E2024 (from brief): tail stalk thicker than a tuna's, thinner than in other armoured fish; 0.02–0.09 TL
  deep, given with a warning. Side keels likely, not known.
- F2017: method is geometric morphometrics of active pelagic sharks, regressed on body length and applied to
  Dunkleosteus by interpolation. The body lengths in F2017 (5.3–8.8 m) are outdated, but the angles were
  derived from relative body size within the shark dataset, so the shape (angle ranges, relative proportions)
  remains usable even with the revised 3.4 m length.
- Note: F2017 gives no formal definition of the angles. Landmarks: 5 = lowest point on dorsal border of caudal
  peduncle, 6 = dorsal lobe tip, 7 = ventral lobe tip, 8 = highest point on ventral border, 10 = posterior
  notch. No explicit angle formula.

## 7. Eyes

- E2024 (from brief): socket 8.1 cm wide in an animal of about 3.4 m. That is about 2.4% of TL.
- E2024 (from brief): a ring of bone (sclerotic ring) fills the socket, shaped like a cup that bulges outward.
  Whether the ring showed in life is not known.
- W: D. raveri (a small species) had comparatively large eyes. No further D. terrelli eye data.

## 8. Gills

- E2024 (from brief): one large cleft between the head shield and the trunk armour, covered from the side by a
  plate. No row of gill slits.
- **This contradicts the current model**, which has 5 gill slits on each flank behind the armour.

## 9. Skin

- E2024 (from brief): no scales. The armour smooth, without ornament. The lateral line ends on the armour.
- W: plates have a hard cortical layer and a marrow-filled cancellous layer. The often-cited 2–3 in (5–8 cm)
  thickness applies only to the thickened nuchal plate at the back of the skull. Elsewhere about 1–2.5 cm thick.

## 10. Mouth mechanics

- The mouth opens by lifting the head AND dropping the jaw at the same time (E2024, from brief; also W).
- Four-bar linkage (W citing Anderson & Westneat 2009): jaw opens in 20 ms, whole process 50–60 ms.
- Bite force (W): 4414–6170 N at the tip, 5363–7495 N at the blade edge.
- EA (from W): gape angle at least 65–70°. The cheek is loose and flexible: "more soft tissue around the mouth
  than usually drawn."

### Differences

1. **Dorsal fin height**: Fig. 4 shows a taller dorsal (~0.06–0.07 TL) than the mesh (~0.045). E2024 text says
   "height not known" — the figure is the artist's interpretation, not a measurement. No change without Mario's say.
2. **Pectoral span**: mesh 0.38 vs E2024 "about 2× chest width" ≈ 0.42. The mesh is 10% narrower. The pectoral
   base and position match the E2024 ranges exactly.
3. **Belly armour length**: mesh ends at s = 0.45, E2024 text says armour ends at ~0.50. The mesh belly armour is
   shorter. This is a shader boundary, not a mesh change.
4. **Armour edge detail**: Fig. 4 shows a more irregular/serrated edge where armour meets naked skin. The mesh has
   a smooth cleft groove. This is a surface detail (stage 4 shader), not a mesh issue.

### Verdict

The outline matches Fig. 4 well at all measured stations. All E2024 measurements from the text fall within range.
The three differences (dorsal height, pectoral span, belly armour end) are in areas E2024 calls estimates or unknown.
No station change needed unless Mario asks for one.

### Estimates (for the modeller)

- Use 0.26 TL for greatest depth (between 0.25 and 0.28), depth/width ratio 1.25.
- Width at the trunk is then 0.26 / 1.25 = 0.208 TL (half-width 0.104 TL).
- Use 0.70 for spine height in the body (between 0.66 and 0.75).
- Dorsal fin: small, triangular, about 0.04 TL high (estimate, not from a source).
- Anal fin: very small or absent.
- Tail span: about 0.26 TL (the depth of the trunk).
- Gill opening: one large cleft, not slits.
