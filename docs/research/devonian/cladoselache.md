## What they say, word for word

W:

- "It was similar in body shape to modern lamnid sharks"
- "The eyes and nasal capsules were large and shifted forwards."
- The eyes have more than 20 small plates of bone around them (paraphrase of the tool; the sentence was not given).
- "The gill slits were separated by muscular flaps packed with fibrous overlapping gill filaments." At least five pairs.
- "There are a pair of large pectoral fins behind the gills and low pelvic fins further back along the underside."
- "Cladoselache has two dorsal fins, with the first being larger and preceded by a stout, curved fin spine."
- "The caudal peduncle (tail) is tapered and keeled, ending at a crescent-shaped caudal fin."
- "Placoid scales (denticles) were present in Cladoselache, but they are tiny and bluntly conical structures"; larger
  denticles on the margins of the fins and near the lateral line.
- "The largest undisputed skeleton of Cladoselache was about 2.0 meters (6.6 feet) in length"
- "Cladoselache is considered to have been a fast-moving and fairly agile marine predator"
- "Some of the fish remains were found tail first within the stomach"
- "The teeth of Cladoselache were slender and smooth-edged, making them suitable for grasping, but not tearing or
  chewing."
- The mouth is terminal, as in the frilled shark (paraphrase of the tool).

H1938:

- The spine "was found in its natural position, almost perpendicular to the body surface of a large Cladoselache".
- "the spine is situated just in front of the anterior border of the pectoral fins."
- From `devonian-models.md` (not read again): about 8 cm high and almost as long, 8 mm thick, in an animal of over
  2 m; no buried root.

ER: "a mouth located at the front of the head (a mouth type called "terminal")"; keels on the tail stalk and a
crescent tail with the upper lobe about as large as the lower.

FW: "strong spines composed of dentine and enamel". Not checked against a paper.
