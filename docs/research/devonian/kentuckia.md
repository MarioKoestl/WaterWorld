upper lobe of the tail.

## What they say

- KGS: Kentuckia "was 4 inches or less in length", one of the small ray-finned fishes of the Late Devonian black
  shales; small fish like these may have schooled around floating logs. The card's 10 cm fits. Which species KGS
  means is not said (the genus is named after Kentucky, where K. deani comes from).
- WP: K. hlavini Dunkle 1964 of the Cleveland Shale is no longer thought to belong to the genus (after Giles and
  Friedman 2014).
- WKSU: slabs full of its scales and skull bones; a prey of the sharks of the Cleveland Shale.
- YPM: a specimen of K. hlavini from the Cleveland Shale, catalogued under Palaeonisciformes, Stegotrachelidae.
- I2026 (caption): the left lower jaw of CMNH 9562 in three views, scale bar 2 mm. `devonian-models.md` gives its
  length (24 mm) and that it is about a fifth as deep as long.
- MN: the caption dates it to the Givetian (Middle Devonian), older than the Cleveland Shale. A whole slim fish with its
  tail.
- MD: a Late Devonian relative from Gogo, crushed flat; the body looks deeper than in MN (about 0.30 TL against 0.22),
  the tail fin is broken.

## Measured on MN

The picture turned by 7.5 degrees so that the fish lies level, a grid of 5 % TL along it and 2 % TL across it, the
middle line through the snout. Read by eye; the outline of a fossil smeared flat is uncertain by about 0.01 TL.

| s (TL) | back above the middle line | belly below it |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0.085 | 0.080 |
| 0.20 | 0.100 | 0.107 |
| 0.30 to 0.40 | 0.110 | 0.110 to 0.115 |
| 0.50 | about 0.10 | 0.085 |
| 0.60 | 0.090 | 0.040 (the anal fin is smeared into it) |
| 0.70 | 0.070 | 0.020 |
| 1.00 | the tip of the scaled upper lobe, 0.13 above | |

So: deepest 0.22 TL at 0.3 to 0.4, the tail stalk about 0.08 deep at 0.7, the scaled axis rising into a long upper
lobe. The places of the fins cannot be read on MN; they are those of these fishes in general (estimate, not
verified): one dorsal fin far back, the anal fin under and behind it, the pelvic fins in the middle of the belly, the
pectoral fins low behind the gill cover.

## Not known

The colour (no pattern is drawn), the width of the body, the places and sizes of the fins of K. hlavini itself, how it
swam and whether it schooled (the school is the card's).
