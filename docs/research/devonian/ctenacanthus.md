Notes for `docs/briefs/ctenacanthus-build.md`. Read on 2026-11-19. TL is the total length, snout to the tip of the
upper lobe of the tail.

## What they say, word for word

Z1981 (on OK):

- "The Paleozoic members of this group are readily identified as having two dorsal fins, each preceded by a basically
  neo-selachian finspine whose apical end protrudes beyond the epidermis"
- "One anal fin is present."
- "The shape of the body is fusiform."
- "The radials of the paired fins and the ventral lobe of the caudal fin are usually undivided transversally"
- The skin is armoured with placoid denticles (paraphrase of the tool).

OK:

- "Ctenacanthid spines were sub-cylindrical and pointed."
- "The fin spines are distinctively ornamented with thin rows of dentine-like material."
- The whole ctenacanth from Texas: "an estimated length of 1.6 m (5 ft) is obtained"; its spines "the anterior 37%
  longer than the posterior one (26 vs. 19 cm)".

KGS:

- The partial body from the Cleveland Shale preserves "leathery skin (shagreen), jaws, teeth, and pectoral fins and a
  partial impression of a fin spine" (after Maisey 1984).
- "Whole spines are 4 to 7 inches (10 to 18 cm) in length"
- "Spines are ornamented with narrow, closely spaced, longitudinal (oriented along the long axis) ridges or ribs."
  Many also carry tiny bumps.
- The sharks were "likely less than six feet long".
- "Ctenacanthus had a shark-like body with two dorsal (upper) fins."

## For the modeller

- Length: the card says about 1.8 m, `world.js` 1.4 to 1.8 m. KGS: likely less than six feet; OK: a whole ctenacanth
  of 1.6 m. `devonian-models.md` names an abstract of 2025 with 4.1 m for C. concinnus: not found again, not checked.
- Not known: the colour, the widths (no view from above), the teeth in detail, how it swam and hunted.
