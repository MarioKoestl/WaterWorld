Correction: earlier notes called the relative only "the Moroccan Amazichthys". Its name is Amazichthys
trinajsticae, a selenosteid (J2022).

## Head and armour (BR2017, from `devonian-models.md`)

- Head shield greatest width given as 1116 mm (CMNH 50319, not full grown), length / width 0.50 to 0.54: about
  0.6 m long, twice as wide as long. Slightly arched, flat, of thin plates (1 to 10 mm).
- Eyes small: a ring of bone about 35 mm across with an opening of 13 mm.
- Lower jaw 610 to 683 mm in these specimens, long and slender, without blades or teeth; the tip bends down and
  inward. Upper jaw plates tiny: 16 % of the length of the lower jaw.
- Median dorsal plate 518 to 544 mm long, about as wide.

## How it fed (C2020 and reports)

- C2020 abstract: jaws "ill-suited for biting and crushing but well suited for gaping ram feeding".
- University of Bristol report (ScienceDaily, 2020-05-20): length likely more than 5 m, lower jaw more than 1 m,
  swimming slowly with the mouth wide open. Written before E2023.
- Report on Heritage Daily (2020): it could not close its mouth completely. Not checked against the paper.

## Length (E2023)

- Wikipedia, "Titanichthys": older estimates 7 to 7.6 m; Engelman 2023 suggested it was comparable to Dunkleosteus,
  about or just over 4.1 m. E2023 itself not read for this.
- At 4.1 m the head shield of BR2017 (0.6 m) is 0.15 of the length; in Amazichthys the skull roof is 135 of 875 mm,
  also 0.15. The two agree.

## Not known

- Everything behind the armour of Titanichthys itself: the body is borrowed from a relative.
- The colour. A search on 2026-11-19 found work on colour from fossil melanosomes in other vertebrates, none on
  placoderms. So the model shows no pattern (Mario, 2026-11-19: nothing without a source).
- The gills and any filter: nothing preserved. Five arches with rakers are borrowed from basking sharks.
- How wide it opened its mouth.
