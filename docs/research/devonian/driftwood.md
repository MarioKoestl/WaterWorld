## What they say

- SD: a log of Callixylon lay horizontal in the Devonian shale, 20 inches (0.5 m) across; about 13 feet (4 m) of it
  was dug out and more stayed in the rock. Its black colour came from the sediment when it turned to stone.
- KGS-C: a log more than 14 feet long and 20 inches across from the New Albany Shale; the grain of the wood is kept;
  a star of shrinkage cracks inside (a thing of the fossil, not of the living wood).
- KGS-L: logs of Callixylon "have been found with attached crinoids in Indiana and Ohio"; some keep microscopic
  fungal threads, and algal spores (Tasmanites) are found on them; algae "would have grown on any log that was at sea
  for any length of time"; no ostracods or other crustaceans, no bivalves, no snails found on such logs; barnacles did
  not exist yet; brachiopods occur with plant debris in these shales, "although not directly" with Callixylon. Small
  fish like Kentuckia may have schooled round floating logs (a guess of the page).
- W: crinoids with a large log of Callixylon in the Lower Ohio Shale; on a log from Indiana a long crinoid stem lay
  lengthwise along the log; pieces of Callixylon floated into the areas of black mud.
- HC: crinoids attached "on the bottom and rarely the sides" of some logs, probably as they floated; the logs at last
  became waterlogged and sank to the black mud.
- H: adult crinoids attached to a floating log "would have hung down beneath the log".

## So

A log 12 m long and 0.6 m thick (the card) is well inside these sizes. Floating logs carry sea lilies hanging from
their underside and a film of algae on their upper side; sunk logs lie on the mud. Nothing else lives on them.

## Not known

The bark of Archaeopteris (not looked up: the bark of the model is an estimate), how the logs broke, the species and
size of the crinoids (estimate: stems 0.4 to 1.1 m, crowns about 0.3 m across), their colour.
